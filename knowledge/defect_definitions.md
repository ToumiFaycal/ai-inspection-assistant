# Defect definitions

What the inspection station calls a good cap, a defective cap, or an empty spot.

## What the station looks at

The camera looks straight down at the cap, so the station only judges what can be seen from above: the top face of the cap and its outline (the rim). Anything on the side of the cap, such as the tamper ring, the threads inside or the side wall, is not inspected. Caps must always be placed top face up.

## The three answers

The station gives one of three answers for each cap:
- **good**: the cap has no defect visible from above.
- **defective**: the cap has at least one of the defects described below.
- **empty**: there is nothing on the inspection spot.

## Good cap

A good cap has a clean top face, a round outline and an intact rim. Some features are normal and are not defects:
- the **gate mark**, a small dot or bump in the centre of the top, left by the plastic moulding process in the factory,
- printed or embossed logos, text and patterns,
- small differences in colour or shine between caps,
- a few **faint handling scuffs**, the light marks any cap gets from being handled.

## Scratches

A cap is defective when its top face has **clear scratches**, or many scratches. A few faint handling scuffs are acceptable, but lines that are clearly visible, deep, long or numerous make the cap defective.

## Dents

A cap is defective when part of its top face is **pressed in**, forming a dent or a deformed area, even a small one.

## Holes, cracks and cuts

A cap is defective when its top face has any **hole, puncture, crack or cut**, whatever its size.

## Bent or crushed rim

A cap is defective when its **outline is no longer a clean circle**: a section of the rim is bent, folded or squashed. A cap that is crushed or heavily deformed is defective too.

## Borderline caps

When it is not clear whether a cap is good or defective, the rule is **"when in doubt, reject"**: treat it as defective and set it aside for a check by hand. A missed defect reaches a customer, while a cap rejected by mistake only costs a second look.
